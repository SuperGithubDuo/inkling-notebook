Installing

The first time, your computer asks if you trust Inkling. This beta isn’t code-signed yet, so Windows and macOS can’t confirm who made it. Only install Inkling from this page or its GitHub release, and don’t install it on a school or work computer without permission.

On Windows
  1. Open the downloaded {{WIN_FILE}}Inkling-Setup-…-x64.exe, usually in your Downloads folder.
  2. If you see “Windows protected your PC”, click More info, check the file name and Unknown publisher, then click Run anyway.
  3. Inkling installs for your account only (no administrator password), adds Start menu and desktop shortcuts, and opens.
On a Mac
  1. Open the downloaded {{MAC_ARM_FILE}} or {{MAC_INTEL_FILE}}Inkling-….dmg and drag Inkling into Applications.
  2. Open Inkling from Applications. macOS says it can’t verify the developer and won’t open it yet. Click Done.
  3. Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, scroll to the message about Inkling, click Open Anyway and confirm with your password. You only do this once.

Your notebooks and the AI, in full
  • Your notebooks are saved on your computer as you write, with a backup every day. There’s no account and no cloud storage.
  • Nothing goes to the AI until you click Allow, not even a check of how many answers are left. Then, when you ask, your question, the part of the page you include and the recent conversation on that page go to Inkling’s AI server and are answered by an AI model (Qwen 3.8 27B) hosted by DeepInfra, with Cloudflare Workers AI as a backup. Inkling’s server doesn’t store them. Both companies say they don’t train on them or keep them after answering, apart from limited exceptions such as support cases and legal obligations.
  • The AI is free, with limits: about 12 answers a day per computer, a monthly limit per computer, and a shared budget for everyone. To share it out, an anonymous installation ID and your internet address go with each question. They’re kept only as scrambled counts: daily counts are replaced every day, and each computer’s monthly count at the end of the month.
  • AI answers can be wrong, so check them. Please don’t send personal information.
  • Under 18? Inkling doesn’t check ages, and clicking Allow isn’t verified parental consent; one Allow covers everyone on that computer account. A parent or guardian should read the privacy notice, install Inkling and decide whether to allow the AI. Children’s privacy rules haven’t been reviewed by a lawyer yet.
  • The app has no ads or analytics. This site sets no cookies and runs no analytics; GitHub, which hosts it, may log visits like any web host.
